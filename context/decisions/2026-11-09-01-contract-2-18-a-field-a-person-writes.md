# 2026-11-09 - Contract 2.18: a field a person writes in shows an example

- **Nov 9 2026: a new clause, 2.18, on placeholders.** The designer, after the nomination form's three questions were found without placeholders (`2026-11-06-02`): "there's no contract on placeholders, and any copy for those would be new, so I'd rather you say whether any should get one. -> please write one up." Written as a clause in `CONTRACTS.md` Part III (Style), after 2.17, scoped to `build` in `contracts/rule-scopes.json`, and the scoped rule files regenerated (`npm run contracts:rules`: `contracts-build.md` holds the clause, `contracts-core.md` has its index line; the other four are unchanged). Adding a clause is a change to the contract files, so it is flagged in the audit (9.4); nothing is loosened or removed.
  **What it says.** A field a person composes text in (a text area, or a text input for a sentence: a reason, a description, a note) MUST show an example as its placeholder, from a source (0.3): "E.g., ..." as the product's inputs already write it (14 of its placeholders do) or, for a note, the question it answers ("Why is this value questionable?"). Where no source has an example, ask; do not write one. A placeholder is never the label and never the only place a requirement is said: it goes when the person types and is drawn at about 2.1 to 1 on white (gray-400; the tertiary text colour is 5.26 to 1), so a format, a limit or "required" stays in the label, the description or the hint, and the label is not restated in it ("Enter contact number"). Each fact once (4.3): an example that is the placeholder is moved out of the description and the hint. Written as copy is (2.3).
  **Exempt, because a placeholder would say nothing:** a field that edits a value the record already holds (the project record form, the card editor, the taxonomy fields); a search, a select and a combo box, whose placeholder names what the control does; a field whose value is a code, a number or a date. Judged from what the product does: of 232 placeholders under `app/pages`, 79 are "Enter ...", "Type ..." or "Search ..." prompts on inputs, selects and searches. The clause does not reach back and rewrite them: it applies to fields that compose text, and to a new field of any kind when it is written (an "Enter your email" under an "Email" label is the restating the clause names).
  **Enforcement: `REVIEW`.** A first occurrence (0.8), and whether a field composes or edits is a judgement from the screen: a count of `<TextArea` without `placeholder` would flag the exempt ones. Named in the clause as a recurring pattern: when a form is restructured or a field replaced, check that the new one kept the old one's placeholder. A check is due if it comes back; it would need a list of the exempt fields, ratcheted like the others.
  **Open, for the designer (0.4), as the clause says.** (1) The two fields that compose text and have no placeholder: the DSA form's Purpose (its guidance is a hint, "Briefly describe how the data will be used and what outcomes are expected.") and the notification form's Sign-off. An example for each is new copy, so none is written. (2) The placeholder colour: gray-400 on white is 2.1 to 1, under the 4.5 to 1 that WCAG sets for text; a placeholder is not exempt from it, and the clause covers the risk by never letting it carry a requirement, but the token itself is the designer's (Figma wins on colour, 2.5) and was not changed. (3) Whether the clause should reach the 79 prompts too, so that "Enter your email" and "Enter contact number" become examples or go.

No code changed by this entry. Not committed.
